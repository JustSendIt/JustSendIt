Data API 🔑

Everything the site shows in public, and everything your own account holds — in bulk, as JSON, for your AI, your bots, your spreadsheets. One key, one door.

⚠️ A key costs a $1,000 burn of $SEND per year — half that for OG Silver, three-quarters for OG Bronze, free and unending for OG Gold and Diamond, for as long as the badge is live. Burning is permanent and buys nothing but this key. Not a reason to buy — just how the door works. Entertainment only.

How to qualify

  • Link the wallet you burn from to this account (read-only signature — never a transaction). A wallet that can’t sign is linked first by the burn proof in Settings → Security (a burn of any amount), and then makes this burn as a separate step.
  • Send $SEND to the burn address 0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD from that wallet. The total across all your linked wallets counts.
  • The burn is read straight from the chain and valued, at the moment you mint, at the lower of the live $SEND price and the median of the last 24 hours of on-chain trading — so a momentary price spike cannot inflate it, and a live price far above the day's median simply refuses to value anything until it settles. When your unspent burn reaches your price — $1,000, or $500 as OG Silver, $750 as OG Bronze — you can mint a key. A burn is irreversible; the price is whatever the day makes it — nothing here is advice about either. A burn backs one live key at a time: the same wallet on another account is refused until that key is revoked.
  • A key lasts one year. Minting spends the tokens that were worth your price that day, from your wallets' unspent burn; what is left stays banked for later years. Renewing takes another burn of your price and adds a year to your current expiry. Revoking a key does not refund a spent burn — so if a key leaks, rotate it: a new secret with the same expiry, nothing spent.
  • OG Gold and Diamond pay nothing: the key is free and never expires, for as long as the Gold or Diamond badge is live. If that badge is ever revoked for selling out, the key stops working with it — except that any time you had already paid for on a burn key still counts, to the day.
  • If the chain or the price can't be read completely, nothing is assumed: you're told to try again, never told you burned $0.

What the key opens — and what it never will

Opens: every public surface of the site in bulk, structured form — profiles, the Send Wall, comments, Send Calls, communities, the all-time board and the Biggest Sender race — plus your own account: your preferences, linked wallets, tracked wallets, watchlist, pins, communities, follows, and your most recent 1,000 points events and 500 each of your posts, comments, Sends and notifications, decrypted for you.

And the market data the site collects: every chain’s TVL, volume, fees, stablecoins and stage with their daily history, the most-traded pools and tokens on each tracked network, new pools as they appear, the New Pairs radar, every token the Scanner reads, Robinhood Chain’s own pulse, the real-world-asset (RWA) tokens on Robinhood Chain with the evidence each is counted on, and how each collector last ran. Every figure carries its source and the time it was read; one that no source has is null with the reason, never 0.

Never opens: anyone else's private data. Other people's emails, linked wallets, 2FA, tracked wallets and preferences are encrypted so that only their owner can read them, and a burn does not change whose data it is. Public data is public on the site already; the key changes the shape you get it in, not what is public.

Encryption, plainly. Every private field is stored scrambled (AES-256-GCM) with a key held outside the database — emails, wallets, 2FA secrets, tracked wallets and your preference blobs. Public content is stored readable, because search, sorting and every wall need to read it. Your API key is stored only as a hash: it is shown once, and if it leaks, rotate it here — the old secret dies, the new one keeps your paid time.

Using it

Send the key as a bearer token. A list is newest-first and pages with before= the next of the page before it; next is null on the last page. limit is 100 by default, 200 at most. 120 requests per minute per key, across every resource. Responses are JSON: a list is { data, next, limit }, a single reading { data }.

curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" \
  "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/posts?limit=200"

A missing, expired or revoked key answers 401, a 121st request inside a minute 429, and a resource that does not exist 404 naming the ones that do — each as { error }.

Market data: chains

Every market figure is an object { v, src, at, why?, label? }: v is the number or null, src where it came from (defillama, l2beat, growthepie, coingecko, coinlore, geckoterminal, dexscreener, robinhood, chainlink, rpc or site), at when it was read (a market cap’s: how recent its source’s figure is — see mcap below), why why v is empty, and label what kind of figure it is. A chain window is 24h, 7d, 30d or custom with from= and to= — whole UTC days, both included; volume and fees add up over it, TVL and stablecoins give its start, end and change.

chains every tracked chain, latest reading
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/chains"
{ data: [ { key, name, chainId|null, gtNetwork|null, logo|null, tvl, volume, liquidity, mcap, fees, stables, tvs, stage|null, isL2 } ],
  at, window, sort, dir, total, notes: [ … ] }

liquidity is DEX liquidity: the TVL of DefiLlama’s DEX protocols on that chain. mcap is the market cap of the chain’s own token, labelled with the token: CoinGecko’s figure, checked against CoinLore. Robinhood Chain’s is null — it has no native token, gas is paid in ETH. A token CoinGecko gives 0 for (it lists no circulating supply) is null with that reason, never 0.

mcap: { v, src, at, why?, label,
  check: { status,          // agreed (CoinGecko and CoinLore within 10%), differs, single (one source), none
    sources: [ { src, v, price, supply, at, relay? } ],   // each source's own market cap, price and circulating supply; at: when that figure is from —
                            //   CoinGecko's own update time (last_updated), DefiLlama's timestamp on its relay, CoinLore's: when this site read it (null: not recorded)
    notes: [ … ],           // what the check found, in words: why two figures differ, a stale or self-inconsistent figure, a coin left out
    mapping: { status, by?, note? },   // confirmed / differs / unchecked: does CoinGecko name DefiLlama's coin as this chain's native coin?
                            //   by: 'gas' when it names only the chain's gas token (Arbitrum: ETH) — the token whose market cap is shown is then not checked
    coin: { id, symbol, name }|null } }   // the token, by its CoinGecko id

The figure shown (v) is CoinGecko’s when it has one — never an average — else CoinLore’s, labelled as the only source. A CoinLore coin counts only when its symbol, its name and its price (within 10%) are CoinGecko’s on the same read; a coin of the same name at another price is left out, and said. A token with no CoinLore coin is said by what was read: “CoinLore lists no coin with the symbol …” only when its walk reached the end of CoinLore’s list, else “… among the first 2,000 coins of CoinLore’s list”. DefiLlama relays CoinGecko’s figure: it stands in (src defillama, relay: true) only when CoinGecko cannot be read, and it never counts as a second source. Chains that share one token (XRPL and XRPL EVM) carry the same figure, and the label says so. Sorting by mcap sorts by v.

Read from the chain’s public RPC — every minute for two days; after that, of each hour, the first row and the first rows with a supply and a bridge reading, kept. pm_* is what the v4 PoolManager holds, claims and fees included — not pool TVL. bridge_eth is the ETH held by the chain’s canonical bridge on Ethereum, not a net inflow. pools_created_* count the pools created since the row before it, at any age: a kept row takes on the counts of the rows removed before it. Market data: tokens, pools and scans

A token window is 5m, 1h, 6h or 24h, as the sources give them; a custom one only where the site’s own pool history covers it, and the answer says where that history starts (for chain=all, network by network).

tokens a chain’s top tokens, or every chain’s
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" \
  "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/tokens?chain=robinhood&sort=liquidity&dir=desc&window=24h"
{ data: [ { chain, address, symbol, name, logo|null, priceUsd, mcap, fdv, liquidity: { v, pools }, volume, change, txns24h, flags: [ … ], scannable } ],
  at, chain, network, basis, window, coverage?: { from }, total, notes: [ … ] }

sort is volume, liquidity, mcap, fdv or change; dir is desc or asc. basis says what the list is drawn from — on Robinhood Chain the 200 most-traded pools on GeckoTerminal, grouped by token — so “lowest” means lowest among those, never on the whole chain. flags: thin (under $10k in its pools), wash (24-hour volume over 100× its reserve — left out of the highest-volume list unless include=all), global-mcap (a market cap counted over every chain); thin and wash are raised only when every one of its pools gave a reserve. A figure summed over its pools leaves out a pool that gave none — never counted as 0 — and then says so in its label, with missing the number left out.

Every chain at once: chain=all puts the lists of every tracked network into one, ranked together — the same sort, dir, window and include, a figure that is missing last in either order, and the 200 highest rows at most, as for one chain.

Each network’s newest finished read, grouped by token on that chain: the same token on two chains is two rows, and chain, chainName and chainLogo say which (the logo is DefiLlama’s chain icon, as a URL). The networks are read at different times — Robinhood Chain’s 200 most-traded pools every 10 minutes, the others’ top 60 on GeckoTerminal hourly — so every figure keeps the time its own network was read; at is the newest of those reads and oldestAt the oldest. networks says, network by network, when it was read (at), how many pools (pools, of at most most) and how often (everyMs); a network not read yet is listed with pools: 0, at: null and why — and named in notes — never left out without a word. stopped says where a read stopped short; stale marks a network whose newest read is over three of its rounds old. A custom window is each network’s own: its daily snapshots start on their own day (networks[].coverage.from), and a token outside its chain’s coverage has volume null with why.

Market data: RWAs on Robinhood Chain

An RWA here is a token an identified issuer issues to stand for a real-world asset — a share, an ETF, a bond or T-bill fund, a commodity fund, a credit or fund share. A token is counted on evidence, never on its name: each carries that evidence (evidence, every item with its tier, kind, text, src and at) and how sure it is (confidence).

  • confirmed — the address is in Robinhood’s Stock Token registry (api.robinhood.com/rhj/assets, tier A1) and its code runs from Robinhood’s token registry contract, read on the chain from its beacon slot (tier A2). A token the registry no longer lists is confirmed only when its beacon slot names the registry and its runtime code is the very code the registry’s own tokens run (compared on the chain) — any contract can write any value into its own beacon slot, so the slot alone never counts; its status says it is out of the registry’s active list. A token of another issuer on this site’s list — Ondo Global Markets, Anchored, ST0x and Midas, by address (tier A3) — is confirmed only once its own beacon (for Midas, its implementation slot) has been read on the chain.
  • issuer-listed — in Robinhood’s registry, its code not confirmed on the chain: not read yet, or read and not running from Robinhood’s registry (then flagged fingerprint-mismatch, and why says what the chain gave).
  • not-confirmed — on this site’s list, or named by Robinhood’s own price, deviation or corporate-action answers though out of its registry; its code not read (or not compared) on the chain yet (why says so). Shown, marked, and left out of every total and every group’s sums. A listed token whose code turns out to run from another beacon (for Midas, whose implementation slot is empty) is not an RWA here and is not listed.

CoinGecko’s id for a token (src geckoterminal when GeckoTerminal’s read gave it, site when it is this site’s own list’s), a Chainlink feed named for its ticker, Robinhood’s other answers naming the address and a beacon slot whose code is not compared yet are shown as supporting evidence (tier B) and never qualify a token on their own. A token with an RWA’s ticker or name and none of this evidence is a lookalike: it is never in the list, it is named on that RWA’s lookalikes, and rwa-token answers for it with a 404 that says which evidence is missing — Robinhood says a token with the same ticker at another address is not one of its Stock Tokens. Fiat-backed stablecoins and tokens merely paired with an RWA are listed apart and never counted. A value it does not know falls back to the default — nothing is refused. With an issuer chosen, the assets view builds each row from that issuer’s tokens only: its main token, figures, premium and chip are that issuer’s token’s. Not paged: the whole view comes in one answer (300 rows at most), and limit and before do not apply. A token with no figure for the ranking is last whichever way the list is sorted; in the tokens view a market cap that is CoinGecko’s every-chain figure is kept out of the market-cap ranking. Connected tokens are ranked by the pool’s reserve (sort=volume: its 24-hour volume), stablecoins by what circulates on the chain (sort=liquidity: their liquidity). reads says, source by source, when it last answered; a read that failed keeps the last good answer, and why says when it failed, why, and that the older one is shown.

kind is the underlying’s: stock, etf, bond (treasury and bond funds), commodity or credit (fund and credit shares). supply is totalSupply() read on the chain. value (“value on chain”) is that supply times the DEX price — not the company’s market value. mcap is GeckoTerminal’s market cap; for Ondo’s and Anchored’s tokens, which live on several chains, it is CoinGecko’s every-chain figure, flagged global-mcap (global: true) and kept out of the market-cap ranking; Robinhood’s Stock Tokens live on this chain alone, and one whose market cap is above its FDV is marked aboveFdv: true (CoinGecko counts its supply differently from the chain) and ranked like any other. It is never mixed with value. liquidity and volume24h are GeckoTerminal’s own per-token figures from its token read (every RWA token is read, in rotation), one measure for every token — GeckoTerminal’s liquidity total comes to less than its pools’ whole reserves. A pool pairing two RWAs is flagged pairsRwa: true; a total takes its 24-hour volume once (pairPools: how many such pools). tvl (“TVL in DeFi”) is the USD of the token deposited in DeFi apps on the chain, from DefiLlama’s per-app deposits, matched by symbol, with the apps in protocols ({ name, slug, category, usd, at, kept?, keptWhy? }: at when that app was read; kept when its later reads failed and its last good deposits are shown, dropped after two days); its at is the oldest app’s read, stale: true past 12 hours, and missingApps names the apps that could not be read, so a figure may be low — a token with no deposits in the apps read while another could not be is “not read”, never “lists no deposits”; caveat says any of that in a few words (“may be low: Morpho Blue not read”), for the line a page shows beside the figure. DEX pools count under liquidity instead. In the assets view a row’s mcap is this chain’s figures only — an every-chain figure among its tokens is in mcapGlobal, never added in, and a row whose only market cap is every-chain is that figure (global: true), kept out of the ranking. A total’s 24-hour volume takes off once each pool between two RWAs that this site has read — the most-traded pools, and the top RWAs’ own pools; its label says a pool it has not read may still be in both tokens’ figures. When GeckoTerminal’s token read fails for a token, its figures stay those of its last good read with that read’s own at (the label says so), or are null with why — “its read at … failed” — never “has not run yet”.

The price checks are made for Robinhood’s own Stock Tokens; for other issuers’ tokens reference, premium, multiplier, halted, paused and mintBurnUsd are null with why. reference.chainlink is Chainlink’s feed for the ticker, read on the chain, with its proxy, heartbeat (seconds) and stale (its last update is older than its heartbeat) — only a feed Chainlink’s directory says prices the tokenised share; “GLD / USD”, which prices the token’s own DEX trading, is not used as a reference (notReference: true, with why). reference.robinhood is the middle of Robinhood’s token bid and ask from /rhj/prices — fields Robinhood does not document. premium is the DEX price against the reference — the price of one token, which is the underlying’s price times the token’s multiplier (Chainlink’s feed prices one token; Robinhood’s token quote has the multiplier applied) — in percent (ref says which reference: Chainlink first, unless it is stale); while the token’s price oracle is paused for a corporate action (paused.oracle) it is null with why — Robinhood says to treat the price as temporarily unavailable then. Stock markets close while DEX pools trade around the clock, so a gap can open outside market hours. deviation is Robinhood’s own published flag for a token 5% or more off the NYSE close for 7 trading days in a row: { pct, direction, days, since, asOf, src, at }. multiplier is how many shares of the underlying one token carries (Robinhood’s currentMultiplier; onchain as read on the chain, and pending with its effectiveAt when a new one is set). halted (Robinhood’s trading halt) and paused.global (every Stock Token at once), paused.token and paused.oracle (read on the chain) are yes/no readings, { v: true|false|null, src, at, why?, absent? }: absent: true means the contract has no such function (the call reverted), which is not the same as “not paused”. mintBurnUsd is Robinhood’s primary-market mint and burn in USD, over a window Robinhood does not document.

evidence items say what the chain was asked: check: "beacon" when the token’s beacon matched its issuer’s, check: "proxy" when only a live ERC-1967 proxy was read (Midas: its code is not compared with the issuer’s); an item on this site’s list that the chain has not agreed with yet has proof: false — listed, not proof. flags: premium (5% or more from the reference, either way), halted, paused, oracle-paused, deviation, pairs-rwa (a pool pairs two RWAs), global-mcap, stale-reference, not-confirmed, fingerprint-mismatch (Robinhood’s registry lists it, but its beacon slot, read on the chain, does not hold Robinhood’s registry). A connected row lists only pools of $1,000 of reserve or more (floor); belowFloor counts the pools left out under it, and unreadReserve those whose reserve GeckoTerminal did not give. Each one’s label says “Not an RWA — paired with $TSLA”, or whichever RWA it is paired with.

issuerProfile is what the issuer says a holder holds, in sentences each with the page it comes from (href) — for Robinhood’s Stock Tokens its documentation’s, with terms the link the tokens’ own terms() gives, read on the chain; for the other issuers one sentence on what this site read on the chain, and that their terms are not read here. controls are the powers a regulated issuer keeps over its tokens — such as replacing the code of every token at once, minting, burning, burning from any wallet, pausing, blocking wallets and changing the multiplier — with each role’s holders and whether each is a wallet or a contract (kind, read on the chain; null before it is). They are shown as powers, not as a verdict: for a regulated issuer they are expected, and they are still powers over your tokens. The Scanner’s health score does not count them. verifiedSource links the verified source (Sourcify) of the code the token runs. Robinhood’s registry is the only one whose roles this site reads: for another issuer powers and roles are empty, and powersWhy and verifiedWhy say so. roles are read from the registry’s own logs (rolesAt); until the walk of those logs is whole (rolesRead.complete), they are the holders up to block rolesRead.cursor of head, and a holder granted a role later is not listed yet. blocked.count is how many wallets it has blocked from moving these tokens (complete once the logs have been read from the start). corporateActions are Robinhood’s for this token, newest first: a dividend raises the multiplier rather than being paid out. connectedWhy is there when GeckoTerminal could not read this token’s own pools, with when and why — never folded into “no pool pairs it”. connectedBasis says where its connected tokens come from: own: true when its own pools were read (one of the 12 RWAs with the most liquidity), else only the most-traded pools this site keeps; note is the sentence for an empty list, with at the read it rests on — a token whose own pools were never asked is never told they could not be read. lookalikes are tokens met in the site’s reads (src: pools, scan or radar) with its ticker or name that are not issued by its issuer. deployer names the registry’s token-deployer role: the Scanner does not raise “serial deployer” on a confirmed Stock Token deployed by it, and changes nothing else.

A token that is not an RWA here answers 404 with { notRwa: true, address, symbol?, stablecoin?, pending?, why, twin?, notes } — why says which evidence is missing, or for a lookalike, for example: “Same ticker as $GME, but not issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) — the registry lists $GME at 0x1b0e…153e”, with the real one in full in twin ({ address, symbol, name, issuer, confidence, registry }). A token with an RWA’s ticker is said as a lookalike first whatever else its own check found — a contract named exactly like a Stock Token whose beacon slot names the registry over other code gets “Same ticker as $TSLA … ” and then what its check found. A twin not confirmed on the chain yet is never said to be its issuer’s: its confidence says so, and why says where this site found it. The token resource carries rwa (the token’s RWA row, or null) and rwaLookalike (this 404’s body for a token that only wears an RWA’s ticker, or null) on Robinhood Chain — also when its market read failed. pending: true means a check that decides it has not run yet (its code not compared on the chain): it is not counted until it has. An address that is not one is a 400.

Where the market figures come from

  • DefiLlama — TVL, DEX volume and liquidity, fees, stablecoins, open interest, which token is each chain’s own, and its relay of CoinGecko’s market caps.
  • L2BEAT — value secured, stage and risks of layer-2 chains, and their daily activity.
  • growthepie — transactions, active addresses, fees and costs per chain. Source: growthepie, https://www.growthepie.com, under CC BY 4.0 — anything you publish from its figures must credit it the same way.
  • GeckoTerminal (by CoinGecko) — pools, tokens, new pools and candles on each network.
  • Dexscreener — search, a token’s links and picture, its pairs’ figures.
  • CoinGecko — the market cap of each chain’s own token, with its price, circulating supply and FDV, and which coin it names as a chain’s native coin; figures that count every chain together, labelled as such.
  • CoinLore — a second, independent market cap for each chain’s token, with its price and circulating supply, to check CoinGecko’s against. (CoinPaprika is not read: its free plan is for personal use only.)
  • Robinhood’s Stock Token API — for the RWAs: the Stock Token registry, quotes (their token bid and ask are fields Robinhood does not document), corporate actions and published price deviations. Its logos are not used.
  • Chainlink — its public directory of the price feeds on Robinhood Chain; each feed’s price is read on the chain itself.
  • Robinhood Chain’s public RPC and a public Ethereum node — the chain’s own readings and its canonical bridge.

Our server reads them, as this site — every request it makes carries the User-Agent JustSendIt/1.0 (+https://sendrh.com), never a browser’s — and your browser never does. Each source is named beside its figures on the Market Data page and in every answer here (src). Their figures are theirs: each source has its own terms, and they apply to whatever you build with them. The chain’s explorer is read only through a keyed endpoint when one is set — never scraped, and with none set it is not asked at all.
