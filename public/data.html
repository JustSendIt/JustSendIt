Data API 🔑

Everything the site shows in public, and everything your own account holds — in bulk, as JSON, for your AI, your bots, your spreadsheets. One key, one door.

⚠️ A key costs a $1,000 burn of $SEND per year — half that for OG Silver, three-quarters for OG Bronze, free and unending for OG Gold and Diamond, for as long as the badge is live. Burning is permanent and buys nothing but this key. Not a reason to buy — just how the door works. Entertainment only.

How to qualify

  • Link the wallet you burn from to this account (read-only signature — never a transaction). A wallet that can’t sign is linked first by the burn proof in Settings → Security (a burn of any amount), and then makes this burn as a separate step.
  • Send $SEND to the burn address 0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD from that wallet. The total across all your linked wallets counts.
  • The burn is read straight from the chain and valued, at the moment you mint, at the lower of the live $SEND price and the median of the last 24 hours of on-chain trading — so a momentary price spike cannot inflate it, and a live price far above the day's median simply refuses to value anything until it settles. When your unspent burn reaches your price — $1,000, or $500 as OG Silver, $750 as OG Bronze — you can mint a key. A burn is irreversible; the price is whatever the day makes it — nothing here is advice about either. A burn backs one live key at a time: the same wallet on another account is refused until that key is revoked.
  • A key lasts one year. Minting spends the tokens that were worth your price that day, from your wallets' unspent burn; what is left stays banked for later years. Renewing takes another burn of your price and adds a year to your current expiry. Revoking a key does not refund a spent burn — so if a key leaks, rotate it: a new secret with the same expiry, nothing spent.
  • OG Gold and Diamond pay nothing: the key is free and never expires, for as long as the Gold or Diamond badge is live. If that badge is ever revoked for selling out, the key stops working with it — except that any time you had already paid for on a burn key still counts, to the day.
  • If the chain or the price can't be read completely, nothing is assumed: you're told to try again, never told you burned $0.

What the key opens — and what it never will

Opens: every public surface of the site in bulk, structured form — profiles, the Send Wall, comments, Send Calls, communities, the all-time board and the Biggest Sender race — plus your own account: your preferences, linked wallets, tracked wallets, watchlist, pins, communities, follows, and your most recent 1,000 points events and 500 each of your posts, comments, Sends and notifications, decrypted for you.

And the market data the site collects: every chain’s TVL, volume, fees, stablecoins and stage with their daily history, the most-traded pools and tokens on each tracked network, new pools as they appear, the New Pairs radar, every token the Scanner reads, Robinhood Chain’s own pulse, and how each collector last ran. Every figure carries its source and the time it was read; one that no source has is null with the reason, never 0.

Never opens: anyone else's private data. Other people's emails, linked wallets, 2FA, tracked wallets and preferences are encrypted so that only their owner can read them, and a burn does not change whose data it is. Public data is public on the site already; the key changes the shape you get it in, not what is public.

Encryption, plainly. Every private field is stored scrambled (AES-256-GCM) with a key held outside the database — emails, wallets, 2FA secrets, tracked wallets and your preference blobs. Public content is stored readable, because search, sorting and every wall need to read it. Your API key is stored only as a hash: it is shown once, and if it leaks, rotate it here — the old secret dies, the new one keeps your paid time.

Using it

Send the key as a bearer token. A list is newest-first and pages with before= the next of the page before it; next is null on the last page. limit is 100 by default, 200 at most. 120 requests per minute per key, across every resource. Responses are JSON: a list is { data, next, limit }, a single reading { data }.

curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" \
  "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/posts?limit=200"

A missing, expired or revoked key answers 401, a 121st request inside a minute 429, and a resource that does not exist 404 naming the ones that do — each as { error }.

Market data: chains

Every market figure is an object { v, src, at, why?, label? }: v is the number or null, src where it came from (defillama, l2beat, growthepie, coingecko, geckoterminal, dexscreener, rpc or site), at when it was read, why why v is empty, and label what kind of figure it is. A chain window is 24h, 7d, 30d or custom with from= and to= — whole UTC days, both included; volume and fees add up over it, TVL and stablecoins give its start, end and change.

chains every tracked chain, latest reading
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/chains"
{ data: [ { key, name, chainId|null, gtNetwork|null, logo|null, tvl, volume, liquidity, mcap, fees, stables, tvs, stage|null, isL2 } ],
  at, window, sort, dir, total, notes: [ … ] }

liquidity is DEX liquidity: the TVL of DefiLlama’s DEX protocols on that chain. mcap is the native token’s market cap, labelled with the token; Robinhood Chain’s is null — it has no native token, gas is paid in ETH.

One row per chain per collection: every run for 7 days, then one a day, kept. fees_24h is the chain’s own gas fees, app_fees_24h what its apps took; src names each figure’s source.

pulse Robinhood Chain’s own readings
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/pulse?limit=200"
{ data: [ { at, block, block_time_ms, gas_price_wei, base_fee_wei, l1_price_wei, min_gas_wei, weth_supply, usdg_supply, pm_eth, pm_weth, pm_usdg, bridge_eth, bridge_batches, pools_created_v2, pools_created_v3, pools_created_v4 } ],
  next, limit }

Read from the chain’s public RPC — every minute for two days; after that, of each hour, the first row and the first rows with a supply and a bridge reading, kept. pm_* is what the v4 PoolManager holds, claims and fees included — not pool TVL. bridge_eth is the ETH held by the chain’s canonical bridge on Ethereum, not a net inflow. pools_created_* count the pools created since the row before it, at any age: a kept row takes on the counts of the rows removed before it. They are null until our new-pools reader has passed the row’s block, and stay null where it did not read every block since the row before (it skips blocks when it falls days behind).

Market data: tokens, pools and scans

A token window is 5m, 1h, 6h or 24h, as the sources give them; a custom one only where the site’s own pool history covers it, and the answer says where that history starts.

tokens a chain’s top tokens
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" \
  "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/tokens?chain=robinhood&sort=liquidity&dir=desc&window=24h"
{ data: [ { chain, address, symbol, name, logo|null, priceUsd, mcap, fdv, liquidity: { v, pools }, volume, change, txns24h, flags: [ … ], scannable } ],
  at, chain, network, basis, window, coverage?: { from }, total, notes: [ … ] }

sort is volume, liquidity, mcap, fdv or change; dir is desc or asc. basis says what the list is drawn from — on Robinhood Chain the 200 most-traded pools on GeckoTerminal, grouped by token — so “lowest” means lowest among those, never on the whole chain. flags: thin (under $10k in its pools), wash (24-hour volume over 100× its reserve — left out of the highest-volume list unless include=all), global-mcap (a market cap counted over every chain); thin and wash are raised only when every one of its pools gave a reserve. A figure summed over its pools leaves out a pool that gave none — never counted as 0 — and then says so in its label, with missing the number left out.

pools every pool reading kept
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/pools?network=robinhood&limit=200"
{ data: [ { network, pool, at, …the base token’s address, symbol and name, the quote’s symbol, dex, price_usd, reserve_usd, fdv_usd, mcap_usd|null, volume over 5m / 1h / 6h / 24h, 24-hour transactions, price change over 1h / 24h, created_at } ],
  next, limit }

GeckoTerminal’s most-traded pools on each tracked network, as read. Robinhood Chain: every read (10 minutes) for 7 days, the first of each hour for 90, then the first of each UTC day, kept. The other networks: every read (hourly) for 7 days, then the first of each UTC day, kept. network is a tracked chain — this site’s key or GeckoTerminal’s id, which is what the rows carry: robinhood, ethereum (eth), solana, base, bsc, arbitrum, polygon (polygon_pos), avalanche (avax), optimism. Add pool= for one pool’s history — a 0x address, or a Solana one exactly as written (it is case-sensitive). A network or a pool that can never match is a 400 that says why. mcap_usd is null where GeckoTerminal has none.

new-pools pools as they were created
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/new-pools?limit=200"
{ data: [ { chain, pool, dex, token0, token1, block|null, created_at|null, first_seen } ],

On Robinhood Chain read from the chain’s own logs — dex is v2, v3 or v4, with the block — and kept 30 days; elsewhere GeckoTerminal’s new pools, with its DEX and creation time, kept 7 days. chain= is one of the tracked chains (as in pools, by this site’s key).

radar the New Pairs radar
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" "https://YOUR-HOST/api/data/v1/radar?limit=200"
{ data: [ { pair, token, quote, …its created block and time, latest price, liquidity, health|null, verdict|null, risk|null, json|null, updated_at } ],
  next, limit, cursor }

The timer’s sweep reads the new pairs and their market figures only, and judges nothing: on a row it wrote, health, verdict, risk and json are null — an older verdict is cleared, never left beside newer figures. They are filled in by a member’s sweep, which runs while someone has the radar open (every 30 seconds at most). cursor is the block the radar has read the chain up to.

scans every token the Scanner read
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_…" \

One row per token, source and ten-minute slot of the clock, with n the scans in it; kept 90 days (the newest 100,000 rows at most), the full json on the newest rows only. chain= is robinhood, ethereum or solana; a Solana address= is case-sensitive.

They run on the live site; a copy of the code run for testing leaves them off. They spend the same per-source budgets as everything else the site reads — GeckoTerminal, shared by the whole site, is asked at most 3 times a minute for them (2 more a minute are kept for people opening a token, and the collectors never use those) — and step aside while the chain’s node is busy with the site’s own reads. A run that fails is recorded with its reason and tried again at its next turn; nothing is filled in to cover the gap.

Where the market figures come from

  • DefiLlama — TVL, DEX volume and liquidity, fees, stablecoins, open interest, native-token market caps.
  • L2BEAT — value secured, stage and risks of layer-2 chains, and their daily activity.
  • growthepie — transactions, active addresses, fees and costs per chain. Source: growthepie, https://www.growthepie.com, under CC BY 4.0 — anything you publish from its figures must credit it the same way.
  • GeckoTerminal (by CoinGecko) — pools, tokens, new pools and candles on each network.
  • Dexscreener — search, a token’s links and picture, its pairs’ figures.
  • CoinGecko — native-token market caps where DefiLlama has none, and figures that count every chain together, labelled as such.
  • Robinhood Chain’s public RPC and a public Ethereum node — the chain’s own readings and its canonical bridge.

Our server reads them, as this site — every request it makes carries the User-Agent JustSendIt/1.0 (+https://sendrh.com), never a browser’s — and your browser never does. Each source is named beside its figures on the Market Data page and in every answer here (src). Their figures are theirs: each source has its own terms, and they apply to whatever you build with them. The chain’s explorer is read only through a keyed endpoint when one is set — never scraped, and with none set it is not asked at all.
