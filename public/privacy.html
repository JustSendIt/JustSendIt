Privacy Policy

Version 2026-09-17 · $Send / JustSendIt · GWCRH@atomicmail.io

The short version.

We store what the site needs to run your account and nothing for advertising. There are no ad trackers and no analytics scripts on any page.

Your email address, linked wallet addresses and two-factor secrets are encrypted at rest. Passwords are hashed. Session cookies are stored hashed. IP addresses are kept only as a keyed hash used to stop abuse.

Everything you post, react to, or pin is public, and a Send Call is permanent by design.

You can delete your account yourself from your profile settings, and you can ask us anything about your data at GWCRH@atomicmail.io.

This page is written from what the code does. Where a feature is optional for the operator (sign-in with Google or X, the Telegram bot), it says so.

3. What is public

Your username, avatar, bio, level, badges, Send Power totals and rankings, follows, posts, comments, reactions, uploads, pinned tokens, community memberships and Send Calls are visible to anyone who can reach the site, including people without an account and search engines. Your profile page and your invite ticket (if you make it public) have their own URLs.

A Send Call is permanent by design: it cannot be edited or deleted once posted. If you chose to attach a wallet address to it, you can detach the address later, but the call itself stays. Deleting a post removes it from the site, but copies other people made or that caches hold are outside our control.

Your email address, linked wallet addresses (unless you attach one to a Send Call or share your wallet yourself), tracked wallets, mutes, IP hashes, two-factor settings and sessions are never shown to other users.

4. Wallets and the blockchain

Connecting a wallet is read-only. To link one you sign a message with it; the message says what it authorises and never moves funds or grants token approvals. We keep the address, not the signature.

Robinhood Chain is a public blockchain. Balances, transfers and swaps of any address on it are visible to everyone, and we read them: to verify that a linked wallet holds what the participation check asks for, to work out holder boosts and OG badges, to watch the first day after a verified buy, and to show balances and swap history on the tracker. We read; we never write to the chain on your behalf.

The swap widget on the home page talks to your own wallet software and to the chain directly from your browser. We do not see your transaction until it is public on the chain like any other.

5. IP addresses and abuse prevention

Your IP address is used in two ways. In memory, it keys rate limits (how many requests, sign-in attempts or uploads one network can make in a window); those counters are not written to disk. In the database, it is stored only as a keyed hash (a blind index) against your sign-up, your last sign-in, community joins, communities you create and posts you write, so the site can notice one network running many accounts. We do not store the readable address, and we do not use it for location or advertising.

The server's own logs record request errors and rate-limit warnings; they are not analytics and are not shared.

7. Other services your browser talks to

Pages load nothing from other companies. The fonts are served from this site. Token pictures, live prices, and the wallet history the tracker and your profile show are fetched by our server and passed on to you, so Google, Dexscreener and Blockscout never see your IP address or the wallets you look at.

The one exception is the swap and wallet-connect flow: while you swap, your browser reads your balance, the swap quote and the transaction status from the Robinhood Chain public RPC (rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com). That service sees your IP address and your wallet address — the same service the wallet app you connected already talks to. Explorer links go to robinhoodchain.blockscout.com; chart and pair links go to Dexscreener. Links to wallets, bridges and exchanges (for example MetaMask, Rabby, Rainbow, Trust Wallet, Coinbase Wallet, Phantom, Relay, MoonPay, OKX) open those services, which have their own policies. Following a link is your choice.

If the site is served through a content delivery network such as Cloudflare, that network sees your IP address and the pages you request in order to serve them and to block attacks.

8. Services our server talks to

The server reads public market and chain data from Dexscreener, the Robinhood Chain RPC and Blockscout (token pairs, prices, liquidity, balances, swap history), a public price feed for ETH, and public stock-quote endpoints for the HOOD ticker shown on the site. These requests carry no information about you: they are about tokens and addresses, made from our server, not your browser. That includes the wallet history the tracker and your profile show, and the token pictures on every page: the services asked see our server, never you. If the operator has enabled sign-in with Google or X, the sign-in itself is completed with that provider. If the operator runs the Telegram bot, messages you send it go through Telegram.

We do not sell, rent or trade personal data, and we do not share it with advertisers or data brokers. We would disclose data only where the law requires it, or to investigate abuse of the site.

9. How long we keep things

IP addresses are never stored — only a keyed hash of each, and each hash is kept only while the check that reads it needs it: the one recorded with a Send Call is erased eight days later, once the same-network ring check can no longer look at it; the one recorded when an account is created is erased after 90 days, so the limit on accounts per network counts the last 90 days; the ones recorded with a community membership, a community you started or an alert last as long as those do and go with them. The site does not keep a hash of the network you last signed in from, or of where a proposal or a vote came from.

Pictures and videos you upload are stored without their location and device data: GPS tags, camera make and model and similar fields are removed from photos (a photo keeps only its orientation), and location and metadata boxes are blanked in videos. A file whose structure cannot be read is refused rather than published as it is.

Sessions expire after 30 days, or when you end them. Wallet sign-in challenges expire after ten minutes. An upload that is never attached to a post is deleted after thirty minutes. Notifications are trimmed so only the most recent of each kind are kept. Rate-limit counters live in memory and vanish on their own. Public content stays until you delete it or your account is deleted. Everything else stays for as long as your account exists.

The operator keeps database backups for disaster recovery; a deleted account can persist in a backup until that backup is rotated out.

10. Deleting your account

You can delete your account yourself from your profile: Settings → Account → Delete account. You will be asked to prove it is you (your password, or a signature from a wallet already linked to the account, or your current second factor). Deletion removes your identities, sessions, tracked wallets, holder state, notifications, mutes, follows, points ledger, uploads and posts; the files behind your uploads and your profile, header and background pictures are removed from disk, and, where the operator has configured it, purged from the content delivery network. Your social handles, IP hashes, age confirmation, balances and preferences are erased from the account. Reactions and votes you left on other people's posts are removed. Community memberships end. A proposal you drafted is deleted; one that was already put to a vote keeps its result, because other members voted on it, but its title and text are replaced with "[deleted]".

What deletion does not undo: Send Calls are permanent by design and remain, shown under a placeholder name of the form "deleted-123" with an empty profile; wallet addresses that verified an account for the participation check or earned an OG badge stay recorded (as a hash) so the same wallet cannot verify another account; and invite codes you handed out that were already used stay used.

If you cannot sign in, email GWCRH@atomicmail.io from the address on the account and we will verify you and delete it by hand.

11. Your rights and how to use them

Depending on where you live you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or export your data, or to object to how it is used. You can see and change most of what we hold on your profile page, end sessions there, unlink wallets there and delete the account there. For a copy of the data we hold about you, a correction we do not offer in the interface, or any other request, email GWCRH@atomicmail.io from the address on your account; we will answer as soon as we reasonably can and will never charge for it. If you think we have handled your data wrongly you can also complain to the data protection authority where you live.

12. Security

Emails, wallet addresses, two-factor secrets and holder-verification detail are encrypted at rest with a key kept outside the database. Passwords are hashed with scrypt. Session tokens are hashed before storage. Two-factor codes are accepted once. Security changes (linking a wallet, turning two-factor on or off, changing the email or password) require you to prove ownership first and produce a notification in your bell. No system is perfectly secure, and we make no guarantee; if you believe there has been a breach involving your data, tell us at GWCRH@atomicmail.io and we will investigate and tell you what we find.

13. Age

JustSendIt is not for children: you must be 18 or older to make an account. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone younger, and we will delete an account we learn belongs to someone who is.
